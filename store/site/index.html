How it plays

Build as high as you can. That is the whole game; the sway is what makes it one.

Tap to drop

A bar slides back and forth over the top of the tower. Tap and it lands. Whatever hangs over the edge is sliced off and tumbles away, and the next bar is that much narrower.

The tower sways

The taller the tower, the more its top moves, and the bar you drop doesn't move with it. A sloppy drop kicks the tower toward the overhang; clean drops let it settle. A good drop times the slide and the sway together.

Slide or Drop

Slide lands the bar where you tap. Drop runs the bar one bar higher and lets it fall onto wherever the top has swayed to by the time it gets there. Same slices, same lives, same slow-to-fast slider; each mode keeps its own best.

PERFECT

Land the seam exactly and the tower calms, the bar flashes gold, and the sliver you lost last time comes back. Chain them and the chime climbs with the streak: PERFECT ×2, ×3, ×4. On iPhone, every perfect taps the Taptic Engine.

Three lives, thirty medals

A miss costs a life; the third miss topples the tower and blows it up. Thirty medals carry across both modes: tall towers at 5, 10, 15 … 100 bars (round coins) and perfect streaks at 3, 6, 9 … 30 in a row (star-edged seals), bronze to gold. Earned mid-run they only chime; they appear on the game-over card.

A world for every season

Nine hand-made worlds, each with its own song. The holiday worlds arrive on their dates; any world can be picked any time from Skins. Every one opens with something happening in its first ten seconds, and its story climbs with the tower.

Sky Islands

The default

A floating island with roots and vines, a storybook countryside far below, up through the cloud sea past drifting islands, into a sunset and a starry night with an aurora.

Toy Kingdom

Any time

Block mountains, a wooden rainbow over a toy-block town, the King's castle, a fort of marching, drumming toy soldiers, a wind-up mouse, and a rocket that blasts off at ten seconds and corkscrews into the clouds.

Synthwave

Any time

Hot pink, neon orange and cyan over a sunset grid and palms. Every so often an 80s driver walks out of the arcade, takes the chrome car for a burnout and donuts, and roars off into the city.

Cyberpunk

Any time

A rainy tech-noir megacity in neon blue, electric purple and magenta: holo signs, flying cars, wet reflections, and a street racer that peels out through a puddle and soaks the umbrella walker.

Halloween

Oct 1 – Nov 1

Small-town Halloween night: porches lined with jack-o'-lanterns, kids in classic costumes, and beyond them the misty hollow, where the Headless Horseman gallops past the moon with a flaming pumpkin.

Thanksgiving

Nov 2 – 30

A cozy small town in a hollow, in a warm comic-strip spirit: a farmhouse porch, a backyard Thanksgiving table with mismatched chairs, a turkey farm, falling leaves, rolling fields and a V of geese.

Christmas

Dec 1 – 27

Snowy Victorian London at dusk: candlelit townhouses, a counting house, gas lamps, carolers in top hats and bonnets, a cathedral dome in the fog, and a horse-drawn carriage on its rounds.

New Year's Eve

Dec 28 – Jan 6

1920s Manhattan in black and gold: Deco spires, searchlights, a mansion party across the water with a green light at the end of the dock, and a real countdown to midnight. The ball drops, the fireworks fly.

St. Patrick's Day

Mar 10 – 18

Spring in the Irish countryside: a thatched cottage with turf smoke, sheep, patchwork fields, a round tower under a rainbow that ends in a pot of gold, and a leprechaun who brings it and keeps watch.

Original music on real instruments

One theme, a waltz in six-eight, arranged nine ways. Seven arrangements are rendered from CC0 recordings of real instruments (saxophone, French horn, euphonium, piano and drums wherever they fit a world); the two neon worlds are built on our own synths (Cyberpunk puts a real tenor sax over them). Every song keeps pace with the game: past 40 bars it speeds up with the slider, time-stretched so the pitch never changes.

Sky Islands
The theme itself: the waltz.
Toy Kingdom
A toy-box march in the spirit of Victor Herbert's March of the Toys (1903): a wind-up key and music box, a toy-piano march with snare and tuba, a horn-and-euphonium trio, a toy battle, a grandioso.
Synthwave
French electro in the spirit of Pyramid's The Hall EP, on our own synths.
Cyberpunk
Slow tech-noir in D minor, after the 1980s tech-noir film scores: a real tenor sax on the theme over our synth bass, pads and gated drums, with distant thunder.
Halloween
The waltz with spooky effects layered on it, like The Haunted Fun House: a werewolf howl, a rattlesnake and a hiss, creaking doors, chains, thunder, owls, a cackle.
Thanksgiving
A jazz waltz in the spirits of A Charlie Brown Thanksgiving (a Vince Guaraldi-style piano trio: bluesy grace notes, block chords, walking bass, a soft kit) and Nat King Cole's "Autumn Leaves" records (a close-miked tenor sax ballad over strings, horn answers). Style only: no Guaraldi tune, no Autumn Leaves melody.
Christmas
A symphonic-pops carol medley in the spirit of Leroy Anderson's A Christmas Festival, built on our theme with our own order and links; public-domain carols only.
New Year's Eve
A 1920s foxtrot/Charleston in the spirit of the Jeeves and Wooster theme (never its melody), a party crowd with clinks, corks and cheers, and a ten-second countdown into "Auld Lang Syne".
St. Patrick's Day
A pub-session set of jigs: whistle, fiddle, accordion, bodhrán, guitar.

Support

Sway Tower is made by one person. For a bug, a question or something you'd like to see, write to montgomerylwhite@gmail.com, and say which device and which world.

Your bests, medals and settings are saved on your device only; deleting the app deletes them. The browser version keeps its saves in that browser.

Privacy

Sway Tower collects no data. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and makes no network calls; it works with Airplane Mode on. Your bests, medals and settings stay in the app's own storage on your device.

When Game Center leaderboards arrive, the app will send only your score to Apple, under Apple's privacy policy; nothing about other players is received or stored. Last updated October 18, 2026.
