## Headline (what is it, in a line)

A one-tap stacker where the tower sways — the taller you build, the more it moves, so every drop is a question of timing the slide and the sway together.

## What makes it special

**Sway is the hook.** Stacking games have been done. Sway Tower changes one thing and it changes everything: the bar you drop does not move with the tower, so a tall tower is a moving target. Lean drops kick it harder; a perfect drop calms it and wins a sliver of width back. Players feel the tower's "mood" and read it like a pendulum. It is the single idea the whole game is built on, and it makes a thirty-second run feel like a balancing act, not a reflex test.

**Hand-made worlds, one for every season.** Nine worlds — seven in a bright flat cartoon style, two in neon — each with its own set-piece in the first ten seconds and a story that climbs with the tower:

- *Sky Islands* (the default): a floating island with roots and vines, a storybook countryside far below, up through a cloud sea past drifting islands, into sunset and a starry night with an aurora.
- *Toy Kingdom*: a block castle, a toy fort whose guards march and drum, a wind-up mouse, and a rocket that launches at ten seconds and corkscrews into the clouds.
- *Synthwave* and *Cyberpunk*: an 80s driver takes the chrome car out for donuts; a street racer soaks an umbrella walker in a rain-slick megacity.
- *Halloween* (the Headless Horseman past a harvest moon), *Thanksgiving* (a cozy hollow), *Christmas* (a Dickensian London street at dusk), *New Year's Eve* (a 1920s Manhattan where a real countdown ends in a fireworks finale, synchronized to the music) and *St. Patrick's Day* (an Irish countryside with a rainbow and a leprechaun who delivers the gold) — each appears on its dates and can be picked any time.

**Original music on real instruments.** One theme, arranged nine ways: seven from CC0 recordings of real instruments — a waltz, a symphonic-pops carol medley, a Charleston with a crowd counting down into Auld Lang Syne, a session of jigs, a toy-box march, a jazz waltz with a tenor sax — and two on our own synths for the neon worlds (French electro, a tech-noir sax ballad). The music keeps pace with the game: past 40 bars the song speeds up with the slider, time-stretched live so the pitch never changes.

**Haptics with intent.** The Taptic Engine fires on the good moments only: a Success notification on every perfect, a heavy tap every fifth in a row, and a thump with two debris taps when the tower finally topples and blows up. Ordinary drops stay silent, so the haptics mean something.

**Two modes from one rule set.** *Slide* lands the bar where you tap. *Drop* releases it from one bar above, so it falls onto wherever the top has swayed to by then — the same slices, perfects and three lives, with a different read on the sway. Each keeps its own best, and 30 medals (tall towers and perfect streaks) carry across both.

**Simple by design.** A title screen with PLAY and two mode cards; while stacking, nothing on screen but the scene, the tower, the bar and the score. No tutorial, no energy, no upgrades, no notifications. It plays in Airplane Mode and collects nothing.

## Why feature it, and when

Sway Tower is a small, finished, original game that rewards a thirty-second try and a thirty-minute session alike. Its seasonal worlds give it a natural slot in holiday collections, and each one is a full scene, not a palette swap:

| Collection | World | Dates (in-app auto-pick) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Halloween games | Halloween | Oct 24 – Nov 1 |
| Thanksgiving / fall | Thanksgiving | the week of Thanksgiving |
| Holiday / Christmas | Christmas | Dec 18 – 27 |
| New Year's | New Year's Eve | Dec 28 – Jan 2 |
| St. Patrick's Day | St. Patrick's Day | Mar 10 – 18 |
| Arcade / one-tap / relaxing | Sky Islands, Toy Kingdom, Synthwave | any time |

The first nomination is for launch (version 1.0, Sky Islands as the lead art). The next update adds Game Center leaderboards (Slide and Drop) and achievements mirroring the medals.
